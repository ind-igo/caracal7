# SHA-256 workload

The third `Workload` and the second bit layout (`workloads/sha256.mojo`): 43 bit columns, 72 families, 20
opening points, 8 public columns. The message lengths are the ethproofs set, 128 to 2048 bytes.

## The idea

A row is one bit position of the 32-bit words (row 0 the top bit), a chain is one round, so `h1 = 32`;
blocks run 64 chains each from chain 0. Every column is a bit. Keccak's three moves carry over: a rotation
is a cyclic read at `32 - r`, the state moves to the next chain as a linear copy with the axis-2 gate, the
round constant rides in a public column. Two things are new.

- **Additions mod 2^32.** A sum is one linear relation per row: addends plus the carry in from the row
  below equal the result plus twice the carry out, with the carry in binary bit columns (three for the
  eight-addend `a'`, one for a copy). The carry into the low bit (the last row) would read the top row's
  carry out cyclically, so a per-row public selector `nb` (zero on the last row) cuts it. The carry out of
  the top row is the discarded overflow.
- **Chain shifts beyond the next chain.** The schedule reads `w` 2, 7, 15, 16 chains back and the block
  end adds the block's input state 63 chains back. The builder now admits any `k2` in `[0, h2)` as a
  cyclic read, like `k1`; the IR always did (`dj2` on G). Reads are written forward (`w[t + 16]` from
  `w[t + 14], w[t + 9], w[t + 1], w[t]`) so the wrap lands on message chains where the selector makes the
  family hold anyway; the block-end read `a[t - 63]` is masked by the `end` selector where it wraps.

The transition is where the two meet: an addition is quadratic (products in Ch, Maj, the sigma xors, the
masked carry in) and the gated transition must be linear, so every next-state word is a same-chain column
`an .. hn` and the copy `a[t + 1] = an[t]` is the gated linear family. Six of the eight are copies with a
block-end addition (`bn = a + end * b[t - 63]`).

## Decisions

- **Idle chains hold the state.** A `lv` selector (dense, one per chain) masks the round: past the last
  block `an = a` and the carries are forced to zero by the ripple, so the state settles and the digest is
  the state on the last chain, the IV the state on the first, both public restriction lines (16 lines of
  32 coefficients). The grid needs one chain past the blocks; `CLIENT.grid(32, 64 b + 1)` pads to a legal
  size (128 B: 224 chains, 2048 B: 2688). The cost is four helper columns (`s0 s1 ch mj`) that a masked
  round needs linear, against 16 columns for a tracked hash state.
- **Dense public columns for the message and the block structure** (`msg lv end K s16`, one value per
  chain) and three one-chain columns for the row selectors (`nb ge3 ge10`; the shifts of the sigma
  functions are rotations masked to zero on the top 3 or 10 rows). The verifier's cost is one product per
  public value, as for Keccak. The statement takes `Params` now (`Workload.statement[p]`) for the one-chain
  period and the 63-chain shift.
- **Products stay in the sums.** Ch is `e f + g - e g`, Maj `a b + c (a xor b)`, a three-way xor `u + v
  - 2 u v` of a helper and a read: degree 2 terms of the sum family, no column each. The schedule's
  `sigma0(w[t + 1])` needs one helper for its first two rotations and one for the masked shift.
- **Booleanity on all 43 columns**, as for Keccak.

## Column order

`a .. h` the state entering the round, `an .. hn` the next state, `w ws wc0 wc1` the schedule word, its
successor and carries, `s0x s0 s1x s1` the sigma helpers, `ab mj ch`, `g0x g0m g1x g1m` the schedule
sigma helpers (x: the first two rotations, m: the masked shift), `ac0..2 ec0..2` the carries of `an` and
`en`, `bc cc dc fc gc hc` the copy carries.

## The row-group form (`sha256g`)

`workloads/sha256g.mojo` is SHA-256 as a row group on the 144-row chain of the RSA and passport statements
(the mulmod ripple needs 525 weight slots, and no CRT trick fits 32 `m1` rows with gcd 1 into 144). A chain
packs four rounds in 32-row slots (rows `15 + 32 q + z`, bit `31 - z`; rows 0..14 and 143 idle, so bit `k`
of slot 3 sits `k` rows above the last row and a Horner scan with scale `zeta` reads the digest as
`sum_k H_k zeta^k`, the mulmod fingerprint's convention). The families are this file's; the geometry
changes:

- A rotation is a helper column, not a cyclic read: its family reads the row `r` above on the slot rows
  `z >= r` (public row mask `ge{r}`) and the row `32 - r` below elsewhere (`qall` less `ge{r}`); ten
  rotation helpers and the two masked shifts. The state moves inside the chain (`qlt`, 32 rows down) and
  across it (`q3`, the next chain's slot 0). The schedule's `w[t + 1]`, `w[t + 14]`, `w[t + 9]` are helpers
  placed by slot masks, so the `ws` sum is same-chain; `w[t] = ws[t - 16]` reads four chains up, off on
  message rounds. The block's input state comes 15 chains up through eight helpers `bx*`. Message words
  sit on a block's first four chains.
- The message is witness; the padding rows (`pfix`, `pv`), the IV (`start` and two nibble columns:
  `a + 2b + 4c + 8d < 16 < 127` decomposes uniquely over bits) and the round constants are public. Every
  public column of the group is a full column in term form (`docs/public-columns.md`); only the selector
  and the masks are checked exactly, the rest are the verifier's own derivation, checked zero off their
  masks.
- Wires: the digest accumulator's ingest terms (`dsel` on the last live chain's slot 3, coefficient
  `zeta^(32 (7 - i) + digest_shift)`) and the embedded digest's (segments A/B/C of the 32 bytes at a byte
  offset, brought onto one chain by transports `t1`/`t2` one and two chains up and `u1`/`v` 13 and 14
  chains up for a block-boundary crossing, coefficients `zeta^(128 + s0)`, `zeta^s0`, 1 with `s0` the bit
  offset of the embedding within a chain's 128 message bits). One accumulator serves
  every group, since a group's selector is zero off it.
- Column cost: 43 + 21 helpers (+ 5 for an embedded digest: the transports and `wd`) per group, pooled across groups; 11 exclusive (the vertex
  cover of the quadratic pairs). The group's last chain is idle.
- A group public column declared for shifts `S` must be zero off the mask for `S`, so `sha256_group_public`
  multiplies every column by its mask; and masks wrap mod `h2`, so the builder refuses a group ending
  within 15 chains of the grid's end, whose backward reads would wrap into itself.

`test_sha256g`: three groups on 144 x 96, four proofs.
